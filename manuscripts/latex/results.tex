\subsection{A held-out acute human time point can be reconstructed under the fixed protocol}

The evaluation architecture is summarized in Figure~\ref{fig:p2f1}\label{call:p2f1}. The protocols separate a globally missing Wound7 time point from transfer to a donor excluded from fitting; both use training-only scaling and compare the predicted distribution with stay-still.
\input{figure_captions/f1}

The finalized held-out Wound7 analysis improved on the stay-still baseline by +49.9\%. Dropping one donor at a time preserved improvement in 3/3 retained-donor sensitivity runs; these runs predict the retained donors, not the omitted donor. The held-out donor transfer analysis also passed in 3/3 folds, with a mean improvement of +75.9\% and a mean pairwise displacement cosine of +0.948. In the geometry audit, the median same-donor temporal distance was 1.967 and the median same-time donor distance was 0.233, giving a time-to-donor ratio of 8.46 (bootstrap 95\% interval 6.02--13.11). The mean displacement cosine was 0.948, with a descriptive bootstrap interval of 0.943--0.954. These results show that the current representation and protocol contain a transferable signal for one interval in this acute human cohort, while also showing that the geometry summary is based on only three donors (Tables~\ref{tab:p2folds} and~\ref{tab:p2geometrytable}; Figure~\ref{fig:p2f2}\label{call:p2f2}).

\input{figure_captions/f2}

The result is deliberately bounded. The donors are healthy volunteers, the wounds are acute experimental wounds and the target is one observed time point. The analysis does not contain a DFU patient trajectory or a time-to-closure endpoint. ``Transfer'' here means transfer across the three specified acute-wound donors under a held-out protocol; it does not mean clinical generalization.

A fixed-seed refit assessed numerical resolution and evaluation sampling. Across 25, 50, 100 and 200 RK4 steps, the target energy distance remained 0.705395 and the improvement remained 49.863\%; the largest discrepancy from the 200-step coordinates was below $6\times10^{-7}$ in root-mean-square units (Table~\ref{tab:p2numerics}). Repeated evaluation at 250, 500 and 1,000 cells per distribution gave median improvements of 48.46\%, 48.39\% and 49.26\%, respectively. Gain exceeded the same-count split-half reference in all 50 draws at each count (Table~\ref{tab:p2sampling}). These results assess one fitted field and the observed cells; the ranges across draws quantify evaluation sensitivity, not additional donor replication or confidence in clinical generalization.

\subsection{Time parameterization changes the held-out result}

The linear days axis failed at the held-out Wound7 target, with an improvement of −18.2\% relative to stay-still. Rank, square-root days and log days met the specified noise-reference criterion, and none of the four tested axes diverged. The result is consistent with a time-coordinate mismatch in which the early wound interval occupies a small fraction of the linear 0--30 day range. It does not establish log days as a universal biological clock; the transform must be prespecified and tested in a design with more independent time series (Table~\ref{tab:p2axes}; Figure~\ref{fig:p2f3}\label{call:p2f3}).

\subsection{Sample-out comparison favours the shared field in this cohort}

Under the common sample-out protocol, shared flow matching had mean energy distance 0.395 and passed in 3/3 folds. The non-neural mean-displacement, nearest-donor and optimal-transport baselines also passed in 3/3 folds. The donor-conditioned model passed in 3/3 sample-out folds but had a higher mean energy distance than the shared field in this comparison. In the six sample-in comparisons, conditioning improved on the shared field in 6/6 cases. This difference between sample-in and sample-out is the central safeguard against interpreting capacity as generalization (Tables~\ref{tab:p2methods}, \ref{tab:p2methodsbydonor} and~\ref{tab:p2insample}; Figure~\ref{fig:p2f4}\label{call:p2f4}).

\input{figure_captions/f4}

The data therefore do not justify selecting the conditioned field as the final model class. They support a more limited statement: the conditioned model can fit known donor contexts, while the shared model had the lowest point-estimate sample-out error in the present three-donor benchmark. Neither result identifies the model class that will work in DFU patients.

\subsection{A second donor improves the two measured points, but k=1 already passes}

With one training donor, mean held-out energy distance was 0.5496. With two training donors, it was 0.3854. The reduction was positive in each of the three training seeds: 0.164 at seed 0, 0.176 at seed 1 and 0.156 at seed 2. The mean-displacement control also improved at seed 0, from 0.5325 with one donor to 0.4186 with two, so the benefit of a second donor was not unique to the neural field. The mean within-configuration training SD of 0.0717 was 3.13 times the mean split-half reference of 0.0229, showing that the reference omits a material source of run variation (Tables~\ref{tab:p2seedcurve} and~\ref{tab:p2seedconfigs}). This makes the aggregate direction reproducible across seeds (Figure~\ref{fig:p2f5}\label{call:p2f5}).

\input{figure_captions/f5}

The same analysis shows why the curve cannot be overinterpreted. The paired donor-block reduction averaged 0.165, with a three-block bootstrap 95\% interval of {[}0.081, 0.315{]}; all three held-out donor blocks improved (Table~\ref{tab:p2curve}). The largest range for a single donor and training-subset configuration across training seeds was 0.368, while the mean within-configuration standard deviation was 0.0717. The run-to-run variation is therefore large relative to some individual improvements. More importantly, k=1 already met the specified energy-distance criterion in all six k=1 comparisons. The number of training donors is not an all-or-none success criterion in this dataset, and the two points cannot determine a plateau or a required cohort size. They only establish that adding a second independent context improved the current aggregate error in this pilot.

\subsection{Three mouse arms expose model-arm heterogeneity}

The all-arm audit gave different results under the same protocol. NDB changed by −1.5\% relative to stay-still and did not beat the baseline. PDB improved by +32.1\%, but its POD7 target failed an endpoint-distance ordering check, warning against a simple interpolation interpretation without proving an off-manifold trajectory. GDB improved by +50.6\% with a POD7 target satisfying that ordering check. In aggregate, 2/3 arms beat stay-still, 2/3 met the noise criterion and 2/3 satisfied the ordering check. Each arm had one animal per time point, and NDB covered 59.7\% of the frozen panel. These arm-level computations are useful for protocol auditing, but they do not establish a mouse biological trajectory, a cross-species equivalence or DFU transfer (Tables~\ref{tab:p2mouse} and~\ref{tab:p2mousecounts}; Figure~\ref{fig:p2f6}\label{call:p2f6}).
