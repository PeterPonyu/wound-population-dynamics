\section{Discussion}
A fitted population field brought day-1 cells closer to the observed day-7 distribution in the acute human wound cohort. Improvement occurred when day 7 was withheld globally and under a separate protocol that withheld each donor. These comparisons support reconstruction of the measured interval in three healthy volunteers, with different information available to the model in each task. Donor transfer can use day-7 observations from other people, whereas global timepoint holdout cannot. Keeping these tasks distinct is necessary when deciding what a successful prediction says about a future dataset or an individual patient.

The learned field is a model of distributions under a specified sampling construction. Independent endpoint cells from the same donor define linear paths and regression targets; those pairings are not observed cell lineages or an estimated minimum-cost coupling. At an ideal regression solution, probability conservation refers to that constructed path measure. It does not establish that an unobserved biological distribution follows the same path. Snapshot ambiguity and mathematical identification theory therefore remain relevant even when a held-out empirical distribution is predicted accurately \citep{weinreb2018,lavenant2021}. The result supports an empirical prediction within the evaluated protocol, while leaving the biological process connecting those observations underdetermined.

Additional biological measurements would constrain a different part of the problem. Lineage tracing relates ancestry to later cell states, whereas RNA velocity derives directional information from transcript processing under a kinetic model \citep{weinreb2020,lamanno2018,bergen2020}. Probabilistic fate mapping and neural stochastic differential equations combine such information with further modeling assumptions \citep{lange2022,vinyard2025}. These methods were not evaluated here, and their published performance cannot be transferred to this wound cohort. Their relevance is that they identify possible sources of information beyond marginal expression distributions. Incorporating them would require compatible assays and a new validation design, rather than only changing the function used to fit the current endpoints.

Normalization to a probability distribution introduces another biological limit. Wound repair involves proliferation, loss, recruitment and matrix remodeling, with experimental evidence for coordinated fibroblast and macrophage responses \citep{rognoni2018,shook2018}. The present normalized distributions do not measure absolute tissue cell counts or distinguish those processes. A mass-preserving field can describe a change in a recovered state distribution without identifying whether it arose through movement of surviving cells, altered population growth or differential recovery during sampling. This explains why a computationally coherent flow can remain incomplete as a mechanism of tissue repair.

The time-axis comparison identifies a consequential modeling choice. Changing the coordinate changes the specified bridges, their target velocities and the optimization weights. It is not simply a relabeling of time along one fitted field. Poor prediction under linear days and improvement under several alternatives show sensitivity within this experiment, but do not isolate interval compression as the cause or identify a universal biological clock. Selecting the transformation after inspecting the same target would make that target part of model selection. A later evaluation should separate selection from assessment, following the distinction emphasized in cross-validation studies \citep{varma2006,cawley2010}. The scientific question is whether a fixed time rule transfers to independent observations, rather than whether one of several rules can fit the present interval.

Donor conditioning improved the fit to known donor contexts without improving the overall held-out mean relative to the shared field. Additional context can increase fitting capacity while also creating an extrapolation problem for an unfamiliar donor. A shared field already assigns different velocities at different latent positions, so different donor-average displacements do not establish that conditioning is necessary. The non-neural predictors also improved over unchanged source, showing that simple displacement rules capture part of the available structure. This makes them useful reference models when evaluating more flexible transport or response models \citep{bunne2023}. With three test donors, the observed method ordering remains a description of this benchmark rather than an estimate of a stable population ranking.

The distance used for evaluation also determines the interpretation of improvement. Energy distance compares distributions through pairwise geometry and has a formal relationship to kernel discrepancies \citep{szekely2013,sejdinovic2013}. Its value here depends on the frozen expression coordinates and training-only scaling. A lower aggregate discrepancy can coexist with errors in a rare subpopulation or in an individual gene that contributes little to that geometry. Representation benchmarking has shown why apparently successful integration must also be assessed for preservation of relevant biological variation \citep{luecken2022}. The present comparison does not establish equivalent performance in raw gene space, spatial tissue structure or clinical outcomes. Those would be separate evaluation targets with their own measurement requirements.

The fitted and evaluated donor measures also differ. Cycling through donor--segment pairs approximately balances training contributions, whereas pooled evaluation weights cells according to yield. Equal-donor-mass evaluation of the same saved prediction preserved the improvement, and each donor separately improved under that pooled time-holdout field. This narrows the explanation that the favorable pooled result is driven only by unequal cell counts. It leaves other weighting choices unchanged, including scaling and fitting, and supplies no additional donor-transfer fold. The appropriate independent unit remains the person, not the recovered cell or the number of times a prediction is reevaluated \citep{zimmerman2021}.

Numerical integration and evaluation sampling were stable over the tested settings for one fixed field. Increasing Runge--Kutta resolution changed the coordinates negligibly, and repeated subsampling preserved improvement. These checks address numerical approximation and finite-cell evaluation, while training seeds revealed larger changes in some individual configurations. The aggregate benefit from a second training donor persisted across seeds, but two training sizes cannot identify a learning-curve plateau or a sufficient cohort size. Overlapping training sets correlate the held-out comparisons, and three donor blocks provide little information about the tails of a population error distribution \citep{bengio2004,vabalas2019}. Repeated computation therefore improves characterization of this experiment without replacing biological replication.

The mouse comparison adds a useful change of setting but also exposes the limits of extrapolation. Its model arms produced different errors, each arm had one animal at each time, and the transferred gene panel had incomplete coverage. Between-time differences are consequently entangled with between-animal differences, while representation transfer adds another source of uncertainty. Acute human wounds and experimental mouse wounds do not provide independently observed diabetic-foot-ulcer courses. The next biological evaluation should preserve the intended donor and temporal exclusions under a frozen representation, clock and error measure \citep{roberts2017}. A longitudinal clinical cohort with authoritative outcomes would be needed to determine whether distributional reconstruction contributes to staging or prognosis beyond describing the sampled acute-wound interval.
